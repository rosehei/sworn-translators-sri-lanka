Official Translation Guidelines

Educational reference for government agencies, embassies, and institutions on how to evaluate and accept sworn translations.

Multi-page translated documents must be securely linked to prevent page substitution or tampering. This is achieved through corner seals — where the translator's official stamp is placed across the edges of overlapping pages.

  • Each page overlap should bear a partial imprint of the translator's seal, so that when pages are placed together, the full seal is visible.
  • This method ensures that no page can be removed, replaced, or inserted without detection.
  • Agencies receiving multi-page translations should verify that corner seals are present and consistent across all page boundaries.
  • The seal imprint should be in ink (wet seal) — photocopied or digitally printed seals are not acceptable for official submissions.

Every sworn translation must clearly state the translation direction on the first page of the document. This declaration establishes the source and target languages.

  • Example: "Translation from Sinhala to English" or "Translation from Tamil to English".
  • The declaration should be prominently placed — typically in a header or as the first line of the translated document.
  • Both the source language and target language must be explicitly named; ambiguous descriptions are not acceptable.
  • If the original document contains text in multiple languages, all source languages should be declared.

A clear end-of-translation marker must be placed at the conclusion of every translated document. This prevents unauthorized additions to the text.

  • The standard marker is: --- END OF TRANSLATION ---
  • This should appear after the last line of translated content and before the translator's declaration and signature.
  • No additional translated content should appear after this marker.
  • Agencies should verify this marker is present and that no text appears after it (aside from the translator's certification).

Every sworn translation must include a formal declaration of accuracy by the translator. This serves as a legal attestation of the translation's fidelity.

  • The declaration typically reads: "I, [Full Name], a sworn translator registered under number [Registration Number], hereby certify that this is a true and accurate translation of the original document."
  • The declaration must include the translator's full name, registration number, and date of translation.
  • This statement carries legal weight — any false or negligent declaration can result in professional sanctions.
  • The declaration should be followed by the translator's wet-ink signature and official stamp.

Sworn translations must bear the translator's wet-ink signature and official stamp to be considered valid for legal and administrative purposes.

  • Signature: Must be a wet-ink (physical) signature — digital or printed signatures are not accepted for sworn translations.
  • Official Stamp: The translator's registered stamp should include their name, registration number, and the designation "Sworn Translator".
  • Some translations may also require a court stamp or endorsement from the relevant District Court.
  • Agencies should verify that the stamp details match the translator's registered information in this directory.
  • Photocopied stamps are not acceptable — only original imprints are valid.

The date of execution (date the translation was completed) must be clearly noted on every sworn translation.

  • The date should be written in a clear, unambiguous format (e.g., "15th October 2026" or "2026-10-15").
  • Some receiving authorities (embassies, government departments) may require translations to be completed within a specific timeframe of submission — typically within 3 to 6 months.
  • Translators should follow the date format requirements of the receiving authority when known.
  • The execution date must be consistent with the translator's signature and should not be pre-dated or post-dated.
  • For urgent translations, the date confirms the turnaround time and can be cross-referenced with the original document's issuance date.
